As a former two-term commissioner and former chief operating officer for Multnomah County, Serena Cruz gained the inside track to winning the District 2 seat from the moment she filed.
Few of the other candidates seeking to represent North and Northeast Portland can match her deep understanding of the county, from its budget to its blind spots. Even outside her work with the county, she has worked in some of the core areas where the county must up its game.

Voters should make the easy call and rank Cruz as their number one choice for the District 2 seat.
As a commissioner from 1999 until 2006, Cruz, 59, was known for her sharpness — both intellectually and at times with board colleagues, leading to an extended period of time in her second term where in-fighting dominated. But while we believe Cruz will strongly champion her views, we don’t expect a repeat of that era based on her professionalism and that of the others on the board.
She brings valuable housing and health care experience, having co-founded a construction company and having served as executive director of the Virginia Garcia Memorial Health Foundation. But her command of the details of county government show why she’s the natural choice. Tapped by former Chair Deborah Kafoury to lead the county as the chief operating officer in 2021, she understands what is driving up costs, what the county must address and why growing the economy is so vital for sustaining county services. All three are critical for stabilizing the county.
Cruz resigned in 2024 after differences with Chair Jessica Vega Pederson over the rollout of services to deflect drug users from jail to treatment, she said. The experience also helped shape her view that the county should consider moving to a county administrator position that reports to the board as a whole, rather than the chair, helping insulate the role from political pressures. It’s a proposal worth exploring as part of the regular charter review process.
As with the chair’s race, we are only endorsing Cruz despite the opportunity to rank additional candidates according to preference. However, the other candidates offer a mix of experience with relevance to the position. Readers can find the candidates’ responses to our questionnaire at oregonlive.com/opinion.

Herman Greene, 52, is a pastor who served on the Portland Public Schools board of directors and would prioritize a better coordinated homelessness strategy, public safety and economic development. His work has centered on connecting people with services, whether it’s violence prevention or economic opportunity. At the school district, he was willing to take unpopular stands to ensure opportunities for students, including providing pathways to military careers and opposing COVID-19 vaccine mandates that he argued would affect Black student attendance.
Bri Williams, 38, is a mental health consultant for preschool-aged children with an advanced degree in social work. She emphasized the need to prioritize prevention of homelessness and economic stability, as well as better, more equitable access to health care resources. She would bring her practitioner’s experience to the role, if elected.
Other candidates include Bruce Broussard, 87, a veteran, entrepreneur and host of the Oregon Voter Digest program; Nathan Ong Norris, 32, a hazardous waste technician and community organizer; Tony Robertson, 55, a manager in the civil rights division for the Oregon Bureau of Labor and Industries; and Nabil Zaghloul, 61, a longtime Multnomah County manager leading community justice, health and human services programs.
But Cruz is simply ready to take on the challenge from Day 1. Voters should send her back to the county to do so.
-The Oregonian/OregonLive Editorial Board
