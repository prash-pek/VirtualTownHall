Voters won’t have an easy time deciding between the two candidates running for Multnomah County judge, Position 2, but they can take comfort in knowing there’s not a wrong choice.
The two candidates, civil attorney Diane Sykes and assistant attorney general Laura Maurer Rowan, emerged from a five-way race in May to November’s runoff election. Both have the experience and temperament to serve as a judge. And their many impressive endorsers reflect the confidence the legal community has in both women.
But voters can pick only one. We recommend Sykes both for her breadth of experience and career-long dedication to protecting the rights of those least equipped to navigate the judicial system.
Bilingual in Spanish, Sykes, 56, started her career as a public defender, often representing Spanish-speaking defendants. She then spent several years working as a legal aid attorney, assisting low-income residents before joining the Oregon Department of Justice as a senior assistant attorney general heading its new civil rights unit.
Now, a civil litigator with her own practice, she is lauded by fellow attorneys for her mastery of the law, ability to dive into complex cases and manage them through what can often be a years-long road to justice.

Rowan, 43, also brings an impressive resume. Currently a senior assistant attorney general in the Oregon justice department, she started in the Multnomah County District Attorney’s office, becoming its first dedicated mental health prosecutor, handling involuntary commitments of those with mental illness facing criminal charges. Her other roles — including a stint at the Portland city attorney’s office — have also deepened her experience with mental health’s intersection with criminal justice. And even with Multnomah County Circuit Judge Cheryl Albrecht supervising the county’s mental health court, that experience would be relevant in any courtroom.
But she lacks the broad experience across multiple areas that Sykes has — a background in criminal and civil law as well as a perspective shaped from working in both the public and private sectors. Along with Sykes’ history of seeking justice for those with the fewest resources, Sykes wins our support.
-The Oregonian/OregonLive Editorial Board
