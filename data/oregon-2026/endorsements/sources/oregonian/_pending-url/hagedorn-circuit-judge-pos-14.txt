The winner of November’s election for Multnomah County judge, Position 14, will oversee divorces, juvenile cases, restraining orders and other highly emotional family-law disputes. While both Joe Hagedorn and Elizabeth Savage offer a range of skills relevant to the position, voters should choose Hagedorn as the best candidate for the job.
Hagedorn, 54, is already doing much of the work. Since 2020, he has served as a full-time referee and judge pro tem in Multnomah County’s juvenile court, overseeing delinquency and dependency cases that make up part of the job. His many years in criminal defense — both as a public defender and in his own practice — have also given him a broad perspective of the situations and conditions that land people in court.
His patient approach and clear communication style — key attributes needed for a family-court judge — came through in an endorsement interview with The Oregonian/OregonLive Editorial Board. Considering the vast majority of people appearing in family court don’t have attorneys, the ability to calmly guide the parties through complicated situations is essential. The unanimous support from the other family-law judges in Multnomah County for Hagedorn also speaks to his readiness for the position.
